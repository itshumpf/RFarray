## 1. Method

Estimator is the project's own `pc/rff/dsp.FrameEstimator`, so slopes are
comparable with the rest of the corpus rather than a second opinion computed
a different way. Frames accepted at `inlier_ratio ≥ 0.6` and
`resid_std ≤ 0.8`, matching `WindowAggregator`'s defaults.

Both files were streamed in full for the frame counts. For slopes, the first
**200 accepted frames per (hour, beacon)** were sampled — 7,000 slopes per
receiver.

**Two limitations that follow from that, stated here rather than discovered
later:**

1. The sample is the *first* 200 frames of each hour, not 200 drawn across
   it. Each hourly median is a snapshot near the top of the hour, not an
   hour-wide average.
2. `FrameEstimator` is stateful — it carries the previous frame's mean phase
   for unwrap continuity. Feeding it a sampled subset means that continuity
   is broken at every gap. The effect on a median over 200 frames should be
   small, but it is not zero and it was not measured.

## 3. The drift floor — the one thing the tail gives that nothing else does

**This is the only genuinely new material in this document.** It comes from
hours 6–19, entirely past the 12,225 s where night 4 ended, so it is outside
the scored region and outside anything the series has ever captured.

Every night in the seven-night set has several beacons up, so any wander in a
slope is entangled with between-beacon differences. B2 here gives **fourteen
consecutive hours of one transmitter seen by two receivers**, which isolates
the wander of a single stream.

Standard deviation of the hourly medians, B2, hours 0–20:

| receiver | SD of hourly medians | × `BETWEEN_UNIT_SD` |
|---|---:|---:|
| s3 | **0.00305** | 1.29 |
| d0wd | **0.00430** | 1.81 |

`BETWEEN_UNIT_SD = 0.00237 rad/sc` (`pc/exp_thermal_evidence.py:129`).

**A single beacon on a single receiver wanders by more, hour to hour, than
the between-unit spread the device-ID work leans on.** That is the number
P3 was supposed to produce and this is the first measurement of it.

It is wander, not trend. Comparing the two halves of the run:

* s3 B2: hours 0–5 median `+0.01232`, hours 6–19 median `+0.01192`, shift **−0.00039**
* d0wd B2: `+0.00644` → `+0.00460`, shift **−0.00184**

Over fourteen hours neither receiver moves systematically. Whatever produces
the hour-to-hour spread is not a slow ramp.

## 4. The receiver gap is beacon-dependent — BUT THIS IS NOT NEW EVIDENCE

**Read the correction at the top before this section.** B1 and B3 only
transmit during hours 0–5, and night 4's scored window (2,400–4,700 s) sits
inside hours 0 and 1. **Everything in this section overlaps the region
already scored as night 4**, so it is a re-derivation of that result on a
wider window and with a different sampling scheme — not independent
confirmation of it.

That it lands close to the prereg's table is a check that the two paths agree,
and nothing more:

| beacon | night 4 §10.4 \|s3 − d0wd\| | this document, hours 0–5 |
|---|---:|---:|
| B2 `28:05` | 0.0096 | 0.0074 |
| B3 `f4:2d` | 0.0259 | 0.0175 |
| B1 `a4:f0` | 0.0534 | 0.0460 |

Same ordering, same order of magnitude, consistently lower here because the
window is wider and the sampling differs. **Do not cite the numbers below as
a second observation of the night-4 effect.**

Median absolute difference between the two receivers' hourly medians, same
beacon, same hour:

| beacon | hours compared | median \|s3 − d0wd\| | min | max |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| B2 `28:05` | 21 | **0.00742** | 0.00020 | 0.01132 |
| B3 `f4:2d` | 7 | **0.01750** | 0.00311 | 0.02811 |
| B1 `a4:f0` | 7 | **0.04599** | 0.00448 | 0.05444 |

**The two receivers agree closely on B2 and disagree by an order of magnitude
more on B1.** If the divergence were a property of the receivers alone it
should not care which beacon it is looking at. It cares a great deal.

The same asymmetry shows in the per-receiver stability. SD of hourly medians:

| | B1 | B2 | B3 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| s3 | 0.00295 | 0.00305 | 0.00392 |
| d0wd | **0.02172** | 0.00430 | **0.01250** |

s3 is equally steady on all three beacons. d0wd is steady on B2 and roughly
seven times less steady on B1 and four times less on B3. Whatever is wrong is
specific to d0wd's view of those two transmitters, not to d0wd generally and
not to those transmitters generally.

**Note this is only six hours of B1 and B3 data**, at the very start of the
run, and hour 20 is a partial hour. Seven hourly medians is a thin basis for
any of the B1/B3 figures above and they should be read as an indication of
where to look, not as a result.

## 5. What would settle it

A pre-registered capture with all three beacons up for the full duration,
with the sampling drawn across each hour rather than from the top of it,
and with the estimator run continuously rather than on a subset. If the
beacon-dependence in §4 survives that, it is real and it constrains what the
cross-receiver generalisation gap in `TWIN_INVESTIGATION.md` §6–7 can be
attributed to.

Until then this is one accidental run, exploratory, and nothing here has been
scored against anything.
